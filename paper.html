Perfume & Fragrance · United Arab Emirates · August 2026
Study record · First edition

Which perfume brands and stores did three AI assistants name when UAE shoppers asked what to buy?

100 shopper questions were run through 3 AI assistants 5 times each, producing 1,500 answers. The question set was derived from 8,102 shopper posts, comments and search suggestions. This record documents the inputs, the method and the observed results.

03 Research question

When shoppers ask AI assistants what perfume to buy in the UAE, which brands do the assistants name, and how much do the results vary with the question, the assistant and the measurement method?

Line collected but not published

claude-sonnet-5 was piloted and excluded from the published comparison. It completed 134 of 500 planned runs (26.8%). Its data is retained and is not scored.

  • Cost per answer in this experiment, shown in the table above.
  • No signed-out consumer surface was available, so this line could not take part in the consumer-surface check.
  • Its 10 most-named brands overlapped 9 of 10 with the brands the three published lines named most.

Overlap measured on the share of the line's own answers naming the brand, not on Shelf Share: at this coverage too few questions reach the 3-of-5 threshold for Shelf Share to be defined.

13 Small consumer-surface check

n = 12 questions per assistant, 36 answers in total. Questions were typed by hand into the consumer apps: ChatGPT in a new Temporary Chat, Gemini signed out, Perplexity signed out where possible, one fresh conversation per question, from a Dubai IP address.

AssistantApp answerAPI baseline DifferenceApp answers
ChatGPT51.9% 72.7%-20.8 12
Gemini37.0% 71.8%-34.8 12

All three36.7% 75.2% -38.5 36

The metric is the share of the entities named by the API runs for the same question that also appear in the app answer. The API baseline is built leave-one-out; reference built from the other runs only, so no API run contributes to the reference it is scored against.

6 of the 36 app answers named no entity and asked a clarifying question instead (ChatGPT 2, Gemini 0, Perplexity 4). None of the 1,500 API answers did so.

Two interface differences were recorded: ChatGPT surfaced shopping cards carrying merchant names, and ChatGPT and Perplexity surfaced map widgets. Neither element exists in an API response.

This is a check on one collection day at 36 answers, against a 1,500-answer API set. It is not a measurement of the consumer apps.

17 Question weighting

The 76 blind questions were weighted equally. No observed data was available describing how often each type of question is asked in consumer AI products, so no other weighting could be applied.

18 Known limitations

  • One product category: perfume and fragrance.
  • One market: the United Arab Emirates.
  • One collection day: 13 August 2026. The record describes that day.
  • Assistant outputs change as models and their retrieval layers are updated. A later run of the same battery is not expected to reproduce these figures.
  • The main board comes from model APIs, not from consumer-app transcripts. The two surfaces were compared only in the small check in section 13.
  • The consumer-surface check is 12 questions per assistant, 36 answers in total. It is a check on one collection day, not a measurement of the apps.
  • 23.2% of returned citations resolved only to a Google redirector host and could not be attributed to a publisher.
  • No observed data describes how often each type of question is asked in consumer AI products, so the 76 blind questions were weighted equally.
  • Anonymous access to Perplexity's consumer surface became gated partway through the consumer check; affected answers were collected by clearing cookies and, in one case, by dismissing the overlay in the page inspector.
  • Five extraction and entity-resolution defects were identified in this run and are listed in full below.

Extraction and entity-resolution defects

DefectEffect
Two spellings of one house scored separately
undercount
 Frederic Malle and Frédéric Malle were resolved as distinct entities. Neither carries the other's questions, so the house appears twice at a lower share than its combined presence.
Retailer and localised storefront scored separately
undercount
 Names such as Bloomingdale's and Bloomingdale's UAE, Samawa and Samawa Perfumes, Jo Malone and Jo Malone London resolved to separate entities rather than to one.
Arabic and Latin spellings of the same entity not merged
undercount
 Entities named in Arabic script, such as دبي مول and هند العود, were not merged with their Latin-script equivalents, so bilingual presence is split across two rows.
Observed-domain list contained misattributions
would have misattributed, now excluded
 The entity-to-domain list assembled during the run mapped several entities to a domain belonging to someone else, including an attraction mapped to a retailer's subdomain and two houses mapped to one storefront. Section 16 applies a stated name-matching rule and reports how many rows it drops.
Product names resolved as entities
undercount for the house
 A small number of product names, such as Acqua di Giò and Terre d'Hermès, were resolved as entities in their own right rather than folded into the house that makes them.
Four of the five split one entity into several, or attach a mention to the wrong row. Each reduces the affected entity's score rather than raising it, so a published share for an affected entity is a floor within this run. The fifth was corrected before publication by excluding the affected rows from section 16.
